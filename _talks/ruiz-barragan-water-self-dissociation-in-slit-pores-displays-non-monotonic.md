### Abstract

Although nanofluidics and chemistry in nanoconfined liquids has emerged as an exciting field, the quantitative impact of confinement on fundamental properties remains often unclear [1,2]. Currently, there is not yet consensus on the impact of slit pore confinement on water selfdissociation [1,2] , namely if this ubiquitous elementary reaction is enhanced, unaltered or suppressed by nanoconfinement. We address this question for well-defined water/graphene slit pore systems that allow us to carefully establish the appropriate thermodynamic conditions for different pore fillings, specifically pressure [3,4]. Anticipating our key results, we show that the energetics of the self-dissociation reaction is very sensitive to even subtle changes of the confinement conditions, leading even to non-monotonic behavior depending on water filling.
